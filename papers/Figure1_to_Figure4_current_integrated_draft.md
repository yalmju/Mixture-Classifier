## 2.10. Physics-guided nonlinear mixture model

The nonlinear model combined spectral gating, calibration-informed pretraining, and supervised learning from measured mixtures. Non-negative least-squares regression first excluded background spectra. The retained baseline-corrected spectra were transformed using the logarithm of one plus the raw intensity without Euclidean normalization, preserving spectral shape and response magnitude.

The multilayer perceptron comprised a fully connected layer with 256 hidden units, batch normalization, a rectified linear activation, dropout with probability 0.15, a second fully connected layer with 64 hidden units, a second rectified linear activation, and a three-unit output layer. A softmax transformation constrained the predicted fractions to be non-negative and sum to unity. Optimization used Adam with a learning rate of 0.0003, weight decay of 0.001, and a fixed training duration of 300 epochs. A weighted absolute composition loss assigned greater weight to minor components.

Before supervised refinement, synthetic mixtures generated from the single-component calibration functions were used for physics-guided pretraining. Predictive evaluation left out one complete concentration condition at a time. Every map and pixel associated with the held-out condition was excluded from model fitting, preprocessing selection, and training-duration selection. Composition error was defined as one half of the sum of the absolute differences between predicted and prepared component percentages.

# 3. Results and Discussion

## 3.1. Interfacial reduction produces protruded gold architectures on cellulose nanofibers

The gold-cellulose nanofiber composite was designed to combine plasmonic enhancement and ink-forming behavior in one material. Cellulose nanofibers first acted as a high-area scaffold that concentrated gold ions at oxygen-rich surface groups. Addition of hydroxylamine then initiated interfacial reduction, nucleation, and outward growth of gold domains (Figure 1a). Continued growth generated clustered structures with nanoscale protrusions rather than only isolated spherical particles. These protrusions and the narrow junctions between neighboring domains were expected to increase the density of high-curvature regions and electromagnetically coupled gaps.

Scanning electron microscopy showed that the gold morphology varied systematically with hydroxylamine concentration (Figure 1b). At [lowest concentration], the product showed [insert observation]. Increasing the concentration to [optimized concentration] produced [insert quantified change in domain density, protrusion development, or uniformity]. At [highest concentration], [insert observation]. Optical photographs and extinction spectra changed concurrently with this structural evolution (Figure 1c). The selected formulation displayed a broad optical response that extended toward the 785-nanometre excitation wavelength. The final analysis should report peak positions, spectral widths, and replicate variability rather than rely on the visible color change alone.

X-ray diffraction confirmed crystalline face-centered-cubic gold through reflections corresponding to the (111), (200), (220), (311), and (222) planes (Figure 1d). Transmission electron microscopy and elemental mapping further showed gold-rich protruded domains associated with the carbon- and oxygen-containing nanofiber framework (Figure 1e). Nitrogen mapping will be interpreted cautiously because residual nitrogen may originate from hydroxylamine-derived species, preparation artifacts, or background.

The planned surface and vibrational analyses will test the chemical sequence proposed in Figure 1a. High-resolution gold 4f spectra will determine whether metallic gold dominates after reduction and washing, while carbon 1s and oxygen 1s spectra will determine whether gold loading alters the electronic environment of the cellulose surface. Complementary infrared spectra will test whether the polysaccharide backbone remains intact and whether the hydroxyl and carbon-oxygen environments change after loading and reduction. These measurements can support interfacial association and modification of hydrogen bonding, but they will not be used alone to claim a specific covalent gold-oxygen bond.

Together, the available structural data support a material in which cellulose nanofibers spatially organize gold reduction and stabilize a protruded plasmonic architecture. Final selection of the optimized condition will be based on reproducible structural, optical, rheological, and Raman criteria.

## 3.2. Recoverable ink mechanics couple direct writing with Raman enhancement

The cellulose nanofiber network must satisfy two opposing mechanical requirements. The ink should flow under the shear generated during writing but should rebuild after deposition to suppress lateral spreading and preserve pattern resolution. Steady-shear measurements will determine whether the optimized ink exhibits decreasing viscosity with increasing shear rate. This behavior will establish shear-thinning, while time-dependent structural recovery after high shear will be required before the material is described as thixotropic.

Oscillatory measurements will define the linear viscoelastic region and compare the storage and loss moduli at rest. A storage modulus exceeding the loss modulus within the relevant low-strain range would support the presence of a weak solid-like network. A three-interval test will then quantify disruption under high shear and recovery after returning to low shear. The recovery fraction and recovery time will be compared across the pristine cellulose nanofiber ink, nonoptimized composites, and the selected formulation. Direct measurements of line width, spreading, edge roughness, and drying-induced dimensional change will connect the rheological properties with actual writing performance.

Electromagnetic simulation provided a complementary optical rationale for the protruded structure (Figure 2). Under 785-nanometre excitation, enhanced local fields were concentrated at protrusion tips and narrow junctions between neighboring domains. Because the experimental ink contains distributions of shapes, orientations, and gap sizes, these calculations are interpreted as identifying plausible hotspot locations rather than predicting the absolute ensemble enhancement.

The optimized material produced concentration-dependent Raman spectra of malachite green and a linear response over the tested working range (Figure 2). Spatial mapping yielded a within-map relative standard deviation of 10.8 percent in the current panel. Before publication, this value will be supplemented with the number of mapped points, the number of independently prepared substrates, and between-substrate variability. The combined data are intended to support a dual-function material claim: the cellulose nanofiber network provides shear-enabled processability and post-writing recovery, whereas the protruded gold structures provide local electromagnetic enhancement.

## 3.3. Spatially written single components can be identified using a conventional classifier

Diquat, thiabendazole, and thiram were separately incorporated into the optimized ink and written as their corresponding letter patterns (Figure 3a). Although the three marks appeared similar under optical imaging, Raman mapping reconstructed each pattern using a distinct compound-selective band. Thiram was mapped at 1370 inverse centimetres, thiabendazole at 1270 inverse centimetres, and diquat at 1570 inverse centimetres. In each case, the Raman image followed the written region with limited response from the unwritten background, demonstrating simultaneous spatial patterning and molecularly selective readout.

Spectra from the written regions retained the principal vibrational features of the individual pesticides (Figure 3b). Thiram showed pronounced bands near 551 and 1364 inverse centimetres, thiabendazole was distinguished by bands near 780, 1011, and 1270 inverse centimetres, and diquat showed characteristic bands near 1174 and 1574 inverse centimetres. Comparison of six spectral regions revealed compound-specific multiband patterns rather than dependence on one isolated peak (Figure 3c).

Principal component analysis provided an unsupervised view of the separation (Figure 3d). The first and second principal components explained 36.8 and 33.5 percent of the total variance, respectively, and separated the three pesticides from blank ink. ## 3.4. Competitive surface response creates a nonlinear mixture problem

The analytical problem changed fundamentally when the pesticides were combined. In a mixture, the measured spectrum reflected not only the concentrations prepared in solution but also compound-dependent Raman response, adsorption, competition for enhancing sites, and local substrate gain. The surface-visible spectrum was therefore not expected to remain directly proportional to solution composition.

Recovery and bias varied throughout the ternary composition space and differed among the three pesticide vertices (Figure 4a). Independent concentration-response measurements produced limits of detection of 1.56 micromolar for diquat, 1.74 micromolar for thiabendazole, and 0.37 micromolar for thiram (Figure 4b), establishing strongly unequal detectability.

Equimolar mixtures directly demonstrated the separation between solution composition and surface spectral contribution (Figure 4c). At 10 micromolar per component, linear decomposition returned approximately 30 percent diquat, 23 percent thiabendazole, and 47 percent thiram. At 33 micromolar per component, the corresponding values were approximately 18, 34, and 48 percent. At 100 and 333 micromolar per component, the reconstructed spectrum was assigned almost entirely to thiram despite equal solution concentrations. Once the spectral evidence of diquat and thiabendazole approached zero, their original concentrations could not be recovered by increasing model complexity.

Non-negative least-squares regression remained useful because it transparently reported which pure-component signatures were visible at the surface. It did not, however, provide a general conversion from surface contribution to prepared composition. The benchmark in Figure 4d showed that error increased from binary to ternary mixtures across all evaluated methods. The multilayer perceptron was introduced because the experiments had demonstrated a reproducible departure from linear additivity, not merely because a more flexible algorithm was available.

Within the low-concentration factorial design, the physics-guided multilayer perceptron reproduced the broad ternary composition landscape (Figure 4e,f). The current analysis versions yield a mean condition-held-out composition error of approximately 12 to 14 percent; one exact value will be reported after the final Figure 4 table is locked. Component-wise concentration estimates at 6 micromolar or higher showed median fold errors of 1.67 for diquat, 1.36 for thiabendazole, and 1.33 for thiram. Predictions within a factor of two accounted for 65 percent of diquat, 87 percent of thiabendazole, and 89 percent of thiram measurements. These data support semiquantitative screening within the calibrated range rather than validated quantitative residue analysis.
